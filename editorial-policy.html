Editorial Standards & Advertising Trust

We aim to make financial assistance easier to explore without selling false hope. Our public-resource content and advertising must remain separate.

What counts as a verified listing?

We distinguish between (1) an official agency/program reference, (2) a directory or nonprofit resource, and (3) an opportunity to investigate. A category or opportunity idea by itself is not a verified grant, offer, or eligibility decision.

Source priorities

  • Official government agencies and program administrators come first for eligibility and application rules.
  • Healthcare directories should clearly identify the underlying provider and whether it is a general directory or specific hospital service.
  • Pricing, benefit amounts, and deadlines should appear only with a source and a date when independently checked.
  • Stale, unavailable, or misleading links should be corrected, redirected to an authoritative alternative, or removed.

No pay-to-play eligibility rankings

Businesses may seek clearly labeled sponsorships, but payment must not change official program rules, an individual's eligibility, or the factual contents of a program listing. We will not imply a sponsor is endorsed by Medicare, Social Security, or other agencies.

Healthcare advertising standards

Any eventual healthcare advertisers should be screened for misleading treatment promises, false insurance acceptance, deceptive lead generation, and inappropriate use of sensitive personal information. Visitors should verify that a clinician or facility accepts their specific insurance before scheduling care.

Corrections and transparency

We plan to review listings and source links as the directory grows. The site does not currently guarantee daily updates or real-time availability. Advertiser traffic, conversion, and reach claims should only be published when supported by trustworthy measurement.
