Which documents might you need?

Last will and testament

Directs distribution of probate assets and can name guardians. State signing and witness rules matter.

Revocable living trust

May assist in managing assets and avoiding probate for assets properly transferred into it. Trust drafting and funding can be complex.

Healthcare power of attorney

Names a trusted person to make medical decisions when you cannot.

Living will / advance directive

Records treatment preferences if you cannot communicate.

Financial power of attorney

Authorizes someone to handle specified financial matters. State law and durable authority terms differ.

POLST / DNR

Medical orders for particular clinical circumstances; these are not interchangeable with a living will and usually involve a healthcare professional.

Frequently asked questions

Can I make a will for free in my state?

Some legal-aid groups and court programs offer free forms or clinics, but a free legally sufficient will is not available from every state government. Use the state finder and confirm witness and execution requirements locally.

Is a living will the same as a last will and testament?

No. A living will addresses future medical care, while a last will generally addresses property distribution after death and other estate instructions.

Can I download a living trust form for free?

Templates may be available from some providers, but selecting, drafting and funding a trust can have significant legal and tax consequences. Free state-provided trust forms are not universal.

Do I need a lawyer for an advance healthcare directive?

Many people can complete official advance directive forms themselves, following their state's instructions for signatures and witnesses or notarization. A lawyer can help with unusual circumstances.

Does Medicare pay for hospice?

Medicare covers qualifying hospice services when eligibility requirements are met. Visit the official Medicare resource linked above for current details.
